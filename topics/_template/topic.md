# Problem statement

What problem or gap does this topic address? Why does it matter to you specifically? One or two paragraphs. A good problem statement is narrow enough that a researcher can tell whether a source is relevant in under a minute.

# Motivating questions

Concrete questions research should answer. Good questions are falsifiable or at least checkable against sources.

- Question 1?
- Question 2?

# Interested in

Explicit inclusion filters. Steer researchers toward the angles, framings, and source types you actually want — especially unusual or contrarian ones that mainstream coverage buries.

- Angle or subtopic to pursue
- Types of sources to prefer (e.g. primary papers, practitioner blogs, forums)

# Not interested in

Explicit exclusion filters. This is how you avoid other people's filters and bias: name the framings, hype cycles, and well-covered ground researchers should skip.

- Angle or framing to avoid
- Ground already well covered elsewhere

# Success criteria

How you will know this topic is paying off (e.g. "a monthly digest that surfaces at least one primary source I had not seen").

# Constraints

Anything that bounds research: time horizon, languages, regions, paywalls policy, effort ceiling per task.
